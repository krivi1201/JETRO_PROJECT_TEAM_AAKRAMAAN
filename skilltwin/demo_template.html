Skill Twin

Retiring masters carry skill in their bodies, not in manuals. Skill Twin learns an expert's acceptable range of movement from wearable sensors, compares a trainee against it, and coaches them in their own language. Here we test the engine against a physiotherapist's ratings, on people it never trained on.

        How accurate it is

        Each rule has its own small model. Every person is tested by a model trained on the other 18. AUC 0.5 is guessing, 1.0 is perfect. Rules whose 95% interval clears 0.5 are the ones Skill Twin coaches on.

        XGBoost (primary)Logistic regression (comparison)

        What each model looks at

        Blind spots

        Four sensors on the left arm and leg cannot judge the head, spine, feet or hands. We mark those rules as not observable instead of guessing.

        How we checked it is real

        A model can look good by accident or by cheating. These checks would expose either.

        Leave one person out

        The yellow person is the test person. The model is trained on the others only, then the test moves to the next person, 19 times.

        Shuffled-label test

        We shuffled the physiotherapist's answers at random and retrained the whole pipeline many times. The grey bars show the accuracy reached with shuffled answers; the amber line is the real result. A real signal sits far to the right of the grey bars.

        Data: A Multimodal EMG Dataset for Exercise Quality Assessment in ICE Environments (Hejda, Sokol, Kotolova, Czech Technical University; Mendeley Data). 19 people, 24 exercises, 4 EMG + 4 IMU sensors on the left arm and leg, every set rated by a physiotherapist.
